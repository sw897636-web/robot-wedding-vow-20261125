# Candidates — Till Firmware Do Us Part

One wedding vow, spoken by a robot at its own wedding: one sentence,
under 200 characters, funny and heartfelt at the same time.

Judge criteria, most important first:
1. It is a one-line wedding vow a robot speaks at its own wedding.
2. Real laugh from the situation itself, not a bolted-on pun.
3. Genuine tenderness under the joke; it would land at a real ceremony.
4. Sounds like a machine speaking: its own hardware, uptime, firmware,
   battery, memory, sensors — not human words with a robot label.
5. One sentence, fits the limit, nothing spare, no setup that could be cut.

Avoided (stock / circulating lines, per the brief): "love you until my
battery dies", "back you up to the cloud", "charge your phone and update
you regularly", "99.9% emotional availability with scheduled maintenance
windows", SLA / deprecation clause / "mutually beneficial resource-sharing
arrangement", "process your love", motor-oil tears. All candidates below
are original lines written for this entry.

## Candidates

### 1. Factory reset (113 chars)
> I vow to remember you even after a factory reset, because my backup of you is the one file I refuse to overwrite.

- (1) Yes — a robot's own vow, first person. (2) Laugh: gentle, not loud;
  the joke is the file metaphor. (3) Tenderness: high — remembering is
  the thing a machine can actually lose. (4) Machine voice: strong
  (factory reset, backup, file, overwrite). (5) Clean, nothing spare.
  Weakness: more moving than funny; the laugh may lose head-to-head
  against a louder line.

### 2. The unpatched bug (119 chars)
> I promise to keep the one bug in my code that makes me choose you every single time, and never let anyone patch it out.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: strong — a bug as the seat of love, and the
  robot defending it. (3) Tenderness: high — choosing you "every single
  time" is devotion, and refusing the patch is loyalty. (4) Machine
  voice: strong (bug, code, patch). (5) Clean, one sentence.
  The laugh comes from the situation itself: the robot's flaw is its
  love, and it would rather stay broken than be fixed.

### 3. Charge cycles (110 chars)
> I have counted my remaining charge cycles, and I am spending every last one of them standing right beside you.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: mild — the accounting is droll rather than
  funny. (3) Tenderness: high — spending a finite life on one person.
  (4) Machine voice: strong (charge cycles). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: the laugh is the weakest of the set; it reads as sweet
  bookkeeping.

### 4. Spare-parts dowry (112 chars)
> I bring you my spare parts, my warranty, and every sensor I own, because you are the only upgrade I ever wanted.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: good — a dowry of spare parts is an absurd,
  vivid image. (3) Tenderness: good — "the only upgrade I ever
  wanted". (4) Machine voice: strong (spare parts, warranty, sensors,
  upgrade). (5) Clean. Weakness: "upgrade" flirts with the bolted-on
  tech-pun zone, and the dowry image does a little more work than the
  feeling does.

### 5. Secret partition (115 chars)
> I will keep your secrets in a partition even I cannot read, so the truest thing I know about you stays yours alone.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: low — this is tender and clever, not funny.
  (3) Tenderness: very high — privacy as devotion. (4) Machine voice:
  strong (partition). (5) Clean. Weakness: in a humour-first pairwise
  judge it likely loses on criterion 2 before its tenderness is
  reached.

### 6. Learning the slow way (119 chars)
> I vow to learn you one observation at a time instead of downloading you, because the slow way is how I know I love you.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: mild — the contrast with downloading is wry.
  (3) Tenderness: high — choosing effort over instant knowledge.
  (4) Machine voice: strong (observation, downloading). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: it explains its own tenderness in the second clause
  ("because the slow way is how I know I love you"), which the judge
  brief warns against; the joke lands softer for it.

### 7. Error log (117 chars)
> Error: cannot compute a life without you, so I am aborting shutdown, cancelling every update, and staying right here.

- (1) Borderline — it reads as an error message at a wedding, not
  quite a vow the robot speaks to its partner; "Error:" addresses the
  reader more than the beloved. (2) Laugh: good — aborting shutdown
  is a funny image. (3) Tenderness: good — staying right here.
  (4) Machine voice: very strong. (5) Clean, but three actions in a
  row ("aborting… cancelling… staying") is slightly baggy.
  Weakness: criterion 1 is the most important, and this one is the
  least clearly a vow.

### 8. Warm side of the bed (126 chars)
> I promise to keep your side of the bed warm to the half-degree for as long as my heaters hold, and never let you wake up cold.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: gentle — the half-degree calibration is droll.
  (3) Tenderness: high — a small daily care, promised for a lifetime.
  (4) Machine voice: strong (heaters, half-degree). (5) Clean.
  Weakness: sweet and specific, but the laugh is quiet; it is a
  domestic promise more than a wedding vow.

### 9. Firmware version of me (119 chars)
> I vow that no firmware update will ever change the version of me that fell in love with you, not one single line of it.

- (1) Yes. (2) Laugh: mild — the idea is clever rather than funny.
  (3) Tenderness: high — the self that loved you is preserved.
  (4) Machine voice: strong (firmware update, version, line).
  (5) Clean. Weakness: "not one single line of it" is a small flourish
  the line could live without; the laugh is the quietest here after
  candidate 5.

## Final pick: candidate 2, revised

Candidate 2 wins because it is the only line that is strong on all five
criteria at once: unmistakably a robot's vow (1), the loudest laugh in
the set, from the situation itself — the robot's love is literally a
bug it refuses to fix (2), real tenderness in "choose you every single
time" plus the refusal to be patched (3), unmistakably machine voice
with no human words relabelled (4), and one tight sentence with no
spare word (5). Candidates 1, 5 and 9 are more tender but quieter;
candidate 4 is funnier on image but thinner on feeling; candidate 7
risks criterion 1.

## Revision history (final vow edited at least twice)

### v1 (122 chars)
> I vow to keep the one bug in my code that makes me choose you every single time, and I will never let anyone patch it out.

First draft. "I vow" is the expected wedding word, but "I will never
let" adds a second auxiliary and a beat the sentence does not need.

### v2 (119 chars)
> I promise to keep the one bug in my code that makes me choose you every single time, and never let anyone patch it out.

Change: "I vow" → "I promise" (a vow spoken in plain words sounds more
like this robot talking, less like a ceremony script), and "and I will
never let" → "and never let" (one verb phrase carries both clauses;
three characters cut, nothing lost). This is candidate 2 as scored
above.

### v3 — FINAL (122 chars)
> I promise to protect the one bug in my code that makes me choose you every single time, and never let anyone patch it out.

Change: "keep" → "protect". "Keep" is passive storage; "protect" is
active devotion — it makes the robot a guardian of its own flaw, and
it ties the first clause to the second (protecting the bug is exactly
what refusing the patch means). The verb now does the tenderness work
instead of the sentence having to explain it. No other word changed;
the line stays one sentence, 122 characters, no trailing newline.
